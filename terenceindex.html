About

Protecting crops with biology first

I am a plant pathologist with a PhD from the University of Ibadan, completed with an IITA fellowship. My work asks how living antagonists and plant-derived compounds can control the pathogens that damage crops and stored produce, and how those options can sit alongside sound crop protection practice.

This site is my online CV and a place to share projects and notes with researchers, students and practitioners in plant pathology, food safety and the wider STEM community.

Research interests

Biological control

Using fungal and bacterial antagonists such as Trichoderma to suppress plant pathogens in the field and in storage.

Plant extracts and metabolites

Screening botanical extracts for antifungal activity and characterising the secondary metabolites responsible.

Plant diseases

Diagnosis, aetiology and epidemiology of fungal diseases, including post-harvest rots of root and tuber crops.

Crop protection

Integrated, lower-risk approaches that protect yield and food quality while limiting reliance on synthetic fungicides.

Doctoral research

Biological control of Lasiodiplodia tuber rot in white yam

My doctoral work addressed tuber rot of white yam caused by Lasiodiplodia, a major source of loss in yam production and storage. I evaluated plant extracts and Trichoderma as biological alternatives for managing the disease.

Pathogen
Lasiodiplodia spp.
Host

Approaches
